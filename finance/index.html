Select the anticipated financing. These are the most common baseline items buyers and sellers can screen before the appraiser arrives. An appraisal establishes value and loan eligibility. It does not replace a buyer's independent home inspection.

VA Minimum Property Requirements

The VA appraisal confirms value and whether the home is safe, structurally sound, and sanitary. VA does not require a standard private home inspection, but buyers should still obtain one.

VA-guaranteed loan
  • Safe accessYear-round access must be available. Private roads or shared drives may require permanent access rights and a maintenance agreement.
  • Roof and structureNo active roof leak, severe foundation movement, failing structural component, or condition that threatens safe occupancy.
  • Electrical and heatNo exposed or makeshift wiring. A safe, permanent heat source must protect living areas and plumbing from freezing.
  • Water and sanitationContinuous potable water, hot water, sanitary facilities, and an approved, functioning sewer or septic system are required.
  • Paint on pre-1978 homesChipping, peeling, or otherwise defective paint on homes built before 1978 generally must be stabilized using lead-safe practices.
  • Pests and deteriorationActive wood-destroying insects, serious dry rot, fungus damage, or deterioration affecting structural stability must be addressed.
  • Stairs, decks, and fall hazardsLoose steps, unsafe elevated surfaces, and missing or unstable handrails or guards can trigger repair conditions.
  • Drainage and hazardsWater should drain away from the foundation. Environmental, flood, soil, or site hazards must be acceptably resolved.
Seller shortcut: Repair active leaks, exposed wiring, unstable stairs, missing safety rails, pre-1978 peeling paint, and nonworking utilities before appraisal. There is no single federal “19-inch” handrail rule for every VA property; the appraiser evaluates the actual fall hazard together with applicable local code.

USDA Guaranteed Loan Requirements

For an existing home, the lender must confirm that the property is in an eligible rural area and meets USDA site rules plus current HUD minimum property requirements.

USDA Guaranteed
  • Eligible rural locationThe address must be inside a USDA-eligible area when the loan is processed.
  • Residential useThe site and improvements must be predominantly residential, marketable, and not designed primarily to produce income.
  • Safe, sound, and sanitaryExisting homes must satisfy the applicable HUD minimum property requirements and be functionally adequate.
  • Working utilitiesElectrical, plumbing, heating, water, and wastewater systems must operate safely and adequately.
  • Roof, foundation, and envelopeActive leaks, structural failure, broken windows, missing exterior materials, or conditions allowing moisture intrusion may require repair.
  • Water and wastewaterPrivate systems must be acceptable, sanitary, and documented. Well-to-septic separation must meet the governing standard.
  • Road accessThe site needs direct, dependable street access. Private-road maintenance rights may need to be recorded.
  • Health and safety repairsConditions affecting occupant safety or continued marketability must be corrected or handled through an allowed repair process.
Buyer shortcut: A USDA appraisal is required, but a detailed home inspection is not. Order the inspection anyway, especially for roofs, foundations, HVAC, private wells, septic systems, crawl spaces, and moisture conditions.
USDA HB-1-3555 USDA appraisal vs. inspection

RHA / Section 502 Direct Requirements

“RHA” is used here for the legacy Rural Housing Administration name associated with today's USDA Rural Development Section 502 Direct program. It is distinct from a USDA Guaranteed loan made by a private lender.

USDA Direct
  • Eligible rural areaThe property must be located in a qualifying rural community and meet the program's site standards.
  • Modest housingThe property must be modest for the area in size, design, and cost, and cannot be intended for income-producing use.
  • Decent, safe, and sanitaryAn existing home must be habitable, structurally sound, and compliant with applicable state and local requirements.
  • Complete and functionalRequired living, sleeping, cooking, bathing, heating, electrical, plumbing, water, and wastewater facilities must be present and usable.
  • Structure and weather protectionThe roof, foundation, exterior shell, windows, doors, porches, decks, and stairs cannot present material failure or safety hazards.
  • Access and utilitiesLegal, dependable access and acceptable water and wastewater systems are required. Shared facilities may require recorded agreements.
  • Environmental and flood reviewThe site is reviewed for floodplain, contamination, drainage, and other environmental conditions that could affect safe use.
  • Required repairsThe Agency may condition approval on repairs or approved rehabilitation work needed to make the property decent, safe, and sanitary.
Important distinction: Section 502 Direct also has household-income, repayment, and program-eligibility rules. Passing the property review alone does not establish borrower or loan approval.
Section 502 Direct program USDA Direct handbook

Conventional Property Requirements

Conventional loans do not use one universal government inspection checklist. The lender, investor, appraisal result, insurer, property type, and local code determine what must be resolved.

Lender / investor rules
  • Value and marketabilityThe appraisal must support the price and show that the property is acceptable collateral for the lender and investor.
  • Safe, sound, structurally secureMaterial deficiencies affecting safety, soundness, or structural integrity can make the property ineligible until corrected.
  • Major systems and utilitiesKnown nonworking or unsafe electrical, plumbing, heating, water, or wastewater conditions may require testing, repair, or specialist review.
  • Complete constructionSignificant unfinished construction or renovation must be completed, documented, or financed through an eligible renovation program.
  • Roof and moistureActive leaks, significant water intrusion, severe deterioration, or conditions affecting insurability and marketability can delay approval.
  • Legal access and property useThe property needs acceptable access, residential character, and supportable zoning or legal-nonconforming use.
  • Appraisal “subject to” repairsIf the appraiser identifies a material deficiency, the report may require completion or a qualified inspection before closing.
  • Cosmetic itemsWorn finishes often do not block conventional financing unless they affect value, safety, structural integrity, insurability, or market acceptance.
Seller shortcut: Conventional financing is often more flexible on cosmetic condition, but it is not an automatic pass for serious defects. Correct active leaks, unsafe wiring, structural problems, major incomplete work, and insurability issues early.

Before the Appraiser Arrives

One checklist.
Fewer surprises.

Start with visible safety, water, structural, and access problems. Program rules vary, but these items create delays across multiple loan types.

  • Keep every utility on and accessible. The appraiser may need to observe water, power, heat, plumbing fixtures, and built-in equipment.
  • Clear attic, crawl-space, electrical-panel, water-heater, furnace, and septic access. Locked or blocked areas can force a return visit.
  • Repair active leaks and visible moisture entry. Address the source, damaged materials, and any structural deterioration.
  • Correct obvious safety hazards. Secure loose steps, decks, rails, guards, floor openings, broken glass, exposed wiring, and missing electrical covers.
  • Stabilize defective paint on pre-1978 homes. Clean up chips and document lead-safe work when a government-backed program applies.
  • Gather property documents. Private-road agreements, easements, well tests, septic records, permits, repair invoices, and engineer reports can prevent underwriting delays.
  • Order an independent home inspection. The appraisal is limited and protects loan collateral. The inspection gives the buyer a deeper assessment of the home's systems and condition.
